Equally spaced numbers can hide inside a set whose gaps look irregular. In 1936, [Erdős and Turán](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1936-05.pdf) asked how many integers could be chosen while avoiding three-term progressions. Roth proved that positive density forces three terms; Szemerédi extended the density theorem to every finite length, with the full proof published in 1975.

The stronger question was already explicit in [Erdős’s 1974 problem list](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1974-27.pdf): could a divergent reciprocal sum force equally long patterns even when density tends to zero? The primes illustrate the gap. Green and Tao reached them through transference, while Furstenberg’s recurrence theorem and Gowers’s higher-order analysis opened different routes through the density problem.

Quantitative decay became decisive. [Bloom and Sisask](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.03528v2) crossed the summability threshold for three terms in 2020; Kelley and Meka later sharpened that bound. Longer progressions required their own advances.

The 2026 OpenAI manuscript reports a summable bound for every fixed finite length, yielding the reciprocal-sum conjecture. Its constants depend on the length. This is a reported resolution, with scope distinguished from independent proof assessment.
