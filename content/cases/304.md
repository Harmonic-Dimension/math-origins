In 1900, David Hilbert asked how far Lie’s theory of continuous transformation groups could dispense with differentiability. One descendant became a theorem about groups that are themselves manifolds. A harder question asks what a manifold can force on a group acting on it. Paul A. Smith’s work on periodic transformations belongs to that second question’s background; the precise first modern formulation is not established here.

Gleason, Montgomery, Zippin and Yamabe supplied the group structure theory behind a reduction to the additive p-adic integers. A hypothetical faithful action would bring entire nontrivial subgroups arbitrarily close to doing nothing. Yang’s dimension obstruction, developed further by Bredon, Raymond and Williams, gave one way to constrain such actions.

Two branches made progress under different restrictions. Repovš and Ščepin controlled distortion through Lipschitz maps. Pardon used the topology of three-manifolds to treat merely continuous actions. The 2026 manuscript reports removal of those restrictions in every finite dimension. Its conclusion concerns Lie structure of the acting group; it does not make every given action smooth.
