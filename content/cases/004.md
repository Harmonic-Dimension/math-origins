Hilbert’s tenth problem had an unexpected afterlife: changing “integer solution” to “rational solution” prevented its famous negative answer from carrying over. In [Hilbert’s 1900 formulation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/71655/pg71655-images.html), “rational integers” meant ordinary integers. A precise theory of computation developed later, making the impossibility of his requested algorithm a mathematical proposition.

[Julia Robinson’s 1949 paper](https://www.math.umd.edu/~laskow/713/Spring17/juliarobinson.pdf) already made full rational arithmetic undecidable. But her definitions mixed universal and existential quantifiers; polynomial solvability asks only for witnesses. The integer breakthrough of 1970 therefore left a distinct inheritance.

Progress did not follow one straight road. [Mazur’s geometric conjecture](https://numdam.org/item/AST_1995__228__165_0.pdf) would obstruct familiar ways of encoding integers inside fractions. Meanwhile, [Poonen used elliptic curves](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0306277v1) to prove undecidability for proper subrings allowing almost all prime denominators. “Almost all” still did not mean the whole field.

The 2026 manuscript claims rational undecidability through a reduction using sequences of solvability tests, without claiming an existential definition of the integers. Editorially, this is an inherited question transformed by developments in logic and arithmetic geometry. Its first rational proposer remains unlocated.
