Nelson’s question grew from an idea that failed. In a [letter reproduced by Soifer](https://www.wfnmc.org/Journal%202003%201.pdf), he recalled trying in 1950 to encompass map-coloring questions within one infinite unit-distance graph. The resulting distance-coloring problem survived the unsuccessful shortcut.

Its name compresses different contributions. [Hadwiger had used the seven-color hexagonal pattern in 1945](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.02385v3) for another covering problem; Isbell found a seven-color construction after hearing Nelson’s question. Gardner’s 1960 column is the earliest printed formulation located in this research.

The apparently elementary question also acquired distinct mathematical settings. [De Bruijn and Erdős](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1951-01.pdf) connected infinite colorability to finite subgraphs. Measure theory yielded an earlier five-color lower bound when color classes were measurable, while [de Grey’s 2018 graph](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.02385v3) finally excluded four colors without that restriction.

The 2026 manuscript claims to exclude five arbitrary colors through a new equivalence involving weak measurable colorings, which allow negligible exceptional unit pairs. It leaves six versus seven unresolved. Editorially, the history combines a failed motivating idea, overlapping attributions and parallel lines of progress with different assumptions.
