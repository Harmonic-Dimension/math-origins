The surprise was that the same interaction could behave differently when the spin changed. Lieb, Schultz and Mattis had established a low-energy obstruction for spin one-half in 1961. Haldane’s [July 1981 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.00076v1), preserved in a later transcription, predicted a gap for integer-spin chains. The better-known published papers followed in 1983.

A rigorous example arrived in 1987–1988. Affleck, Kennedy, Lieb and Tasaki constructed a gapped spin-one chain from valence bonds. Its interaction included a squared term, so it left the pure Heisenberg question unresolved. Stability under small perturbations widened that example’s reach without settling the pure point.

Numerics followed another branch. [White and Huse](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.48.3844) estimated the pure-model gap and exposed effective spins at open ends. Later symmetry and index theories clarified the topological question. In 2024, Tasaki proved a nontrivial index conditional on a uniform boundary-field gap.

The 2026 OpenAI release reports both an even-periodic gap and a separate fixed-field result that supplies this boundary hypothesis. Their precise formulations matter; independent proof assessment remains separate.
