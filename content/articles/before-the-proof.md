A proof gives an answer. A mathematical question also asks us to look backward: what made this question worth asking, and what had to become imaginable before anyone could formulate it?

The speed of producing a new argument is one part of that story. The development of a problem is another. Definitions, examples, failed approaches and changes in perspective can all become part of the inheritance carried by a short conjecture statement. Understanding that inheritance can deepen our understanding of the result itself.

## A question is not a fixed object

For this project, we want to keep an original formulation beside the modern one. A name can stay the same while assumptions, objects or ambitions change. Sometimes a broader question becomes tractable in a special setting. Sometimes an improvement in a bound matters without settling the exact value. Sometimes a counterexample changes what the next question ought to be.

These are possibilities to investigate, rather than histories to assign in advance. Each account should explain what its evidence supports and where the record remains incomplete.

## The work of asking

Attribution should make room for more than the name attached to a conjecture. We want to identify contributions to concepts, formulations, reformulations and partial results, while resisting the temptation to turn every question into a single-person origin story.

That takes care. An early paper may use unfamiliar language. A later source may compress several contributions into a convenient name. Claims of priority can be uncertain. When an account cannot identify a first formulation confidently, it should say so.

## Another milestone in the conversation

OpenAI’s release provides the starting collection for this site. It does not, by itself, supply a historical account or establish independent validation of a proof. We keep the reported claim, its precise scope and the evidence for earlier mathematical work visible as separate parts of each case.

This is an intellectually generous project. Interest in what a new tool can do belongs alongside interest in the people and ideas that made the questions possible. Looking before the proof can help us appreciate both.
